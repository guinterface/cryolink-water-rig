## Buy locally (Palo Alto Hardware, 875 Alma St, or PRL; prices UNVERIFIED)
- The fasteners above, if cheaper locally.
- 4 compliance grommets (ID ~3.5, OD ~16.5 mm), 0.8 mm pull cord, zip ties, 4.7 kOhm resistor, headers.
- 5 V servo supply (checked 11-06):
  - SG-5010: 4.8-6 V; no current figure from Adafruit or TowerPro (MEASURED absence). Adafruit's 815 guide: "Some High-torque servos will draw more than 1A each under load" (MEASURED).
  - First choice: a lab bench supply at 5.0 V, current limit ~3 A, so a jam trips the limit instead of a part (ESTIMATE).
  - Fallback (re-checked 11-07, MEASURED): Adafruit 1466 5 V 4 A brick, $14.95, InStock, plug "5.5mm OD, 2.1mm ID positive tip". Adafruit 368 terminal adapter is still OutOfStock.
  - Buy with the brick: Adafruit 373 breadboard 2.1 mm DC jack, $0.95, InStock. It matches 1466. Solder two wires from it to the 815 V+ terminal: "The pin at the end of the jack is the positive pin, the pin in the 'middle' of the jack is the negative pin" (MEASURED). Add both to the Adafruit cart only if no lab bench supply is available: +$15.90.
  - The brick has no adjustable current limit (4 A max). With it, a jammed servo is not cut off, so watch the first dry runs (ESTIMATE). A lab bench supply with a 3 A limit stays the first choice.
  - Add a 470 uF or larger capacitor in the 815's capacitor slot: Adafruit suggests n x 100 uF, "eg 470uF or more for 5 servos" (MEASURED). Ask Lab64 or PRL.
- Waste bucket: borrow (holds ~3.5 L over 20 cycles, COMPUTED).
